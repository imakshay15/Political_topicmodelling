Finding 1 — Ideological Polarity
Topic Divergence · High Significance
The two leaders occupy diametrically opposed rhetorical universes. Pinarayi's discourse is rooted in administrative secularism — assembly proceedings, public welfare, health service, and education — reflective of Kerala's LDF governance model. Yogi's vocabulary is saturated with civilisational Hindu vocabulary: "dharma," "ramayana," "lord," "sanatan," "inspire" — terms entirely absent from Pinarayi's corpus. This is not mere political difference; it represents fundamentally different ontologies of governance.

Finding 2 — Cross-Referential Counter-Narrative Strategy
Mutual Naming · Strategic Signalling
A striking LDA finding: "adityanath" appears in Pinarayi's Topic 0 (0.021) and Topic 3 (0.021) — meaning Pinarayi actively names and critiques Yogi in his speeches. Likewise, "kerala" appears prominently in Yogi's Topic 1 (0.022). Both leaders use each other's states as ideological contrast cases — Kerala is Yogi's "failed Left experiment"; UP under Yogi is Pinarayi's "communal threat." This mutual referencing reveals engagement in the same national conversation from opposing poles.

Finding 3 — Vocabulary Asymmetry (391 vs 128)
Corpus Size Disparity · Thematic Breadth
Yogi's corpus contains 3× more unique vocabulary than Pinarayi's (391 vs 128 unique words). This suggests Yogi addresses a broader thematic range — from religious texts (Ramayana) to national security (Pakistan) to governance (minister, government, chief) — while Pinarayi remains more narrowly focused on Kerala-specific administration and targeted BJP critique. Alternatively, the underlying Yogi dataset may simply contain more raw text. Either way, Yogi's rhetorical reach is significantly wider in the modelled corpus.

Finding 4 — Convergence Zones & Contested Geographies
57 Shared Words · Battleground States
Of 57 shared words, the most politically significant are "kashmir," "bengal," "uttar pradesh," and "state." These are contested political territories where Left and BJP governance models have historically clashed — West Bengal's 2021 election, Kashmir's Article 370 revocation. The shared discourse around these geographies confirms both leaders are engaged in the same national political conversation, but approaching it from irreconcilably different ideological positions.
